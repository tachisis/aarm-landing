What is this?

People are more varied than any job description can capture, and we have met many excellent colleagues quite by chance. This section is therefore devoted to biographical profiles rather than narrowly defined roles. If you feel you fit one of them, do get in touch — we would very likely be happy to have a conversation. If you are looking for a specific, advertised position, please visit the Jobs page.

Non-Jobs

Writer or Critic of ‘Low’ Genre Fiction

Perhaps it all began as fanfiction, a hundred thousand words in someone else's universe, workshopped in forums with people you have never met, received with more careful attention than most literary journals would offer. Perhaps you moved on to fantasy romance, or young adult, or the kind of thriller that serious people describe as a guilty pleasure while consuming it in a single sitting.

Along the way you became genuinely skilled at things the literary world pretends are simple: pacing, voice, the architecture of a series, and the particular craft of writing for an audience that will tell you, immediately and at length, exactly what they think. You also developed an unusually clear understanding of what people actually want from a story, as opposed to what they claim to want.

You may have tried to import this into more respectable work and found the reception lukewarm. The people who have not written two million words of fiction tend to have strong opinions about which two million words count.

We are less interested in the respectability of the canon than in whether you understand narrative, audience, and the mechanics of why something works. If you do, and you are looking for something to apply that to, write to us at nano@aicompanny.ai. Tell us what you have written, what you have read, and what you think most people get wrong about the genre

Failed Startup C-Level Person

At some point there was a launch, a deck that genuinely excited people, and a period during which you were introduced at parties as someone doing something interesting. You hired well, made some calls you still think were correct, and a few you have since revised.

Then came the pivot, or the bridge round that did not close, or simply the slow realisation that the market was not quite where you had placed it.

Since then you have been in an odd position. Operators want a track record of scale you do not have. Investors want you on the other side of the table. Other founders are either too early to need you or too late to want the reminder. You have considered going back into a large organisation and found the idea absurd.

What you do have is an unusual combination: you have held the strategy and the spreadsheet at the same time, you have fired someone and fixed the deployment on the same afternoon, and you have learnt, at some cost, what early looks like from the inside.

If this is roughly your situation, write to us at nano@aicompanny.ai. Tell us what you built, what went wrong, and what you think you understand now that you did not before. We have found that people who have been through this tend to be unusually useful.

Unemployable Generalist Manager from a Hands-On Role

In your life, you have tried a great many things. At one point you were exploring ways to automate bird-watching; later you moved on to simulating wind turbines, while also tinkering with synthesiser engines on the side. In each case, you were launching something distinctly curious and, quite reasonably, concluded that you might be suited to leading projects that demand originality of thought, resilience in the face of uncertain timelines, and a strong network of capable people.

Regrettably, you discovered that many engineering firms insist that R&D must neatly follow SCRUM, that you are not quite cheerful enough for their tastes, and that you are still regarded primarily as a specialist.

If this sounds familiar, write to us at nano@aicompanny.ai. Tell us about your projects, your career, and your story. We may find we get on rather well.

Analytical Creative Who Has Nothing More To Do

Perhaps you have spent your life studying grids, only to realise they are ten different things all called by the same name. Perhaps you worked in infographics and learnt the craft inside out. Or perhaps you are devoted to analytical composition, with a slightly overstuffed El Lissitzky archive at home.

Either way, you now look at the world rather grimly. Landing pages all blur into one, UX feels like a relic in the age of superapps, and enterprise work asks you to communicate endlessly while producing something painfully derivative.

You visited our website and thought it was trashy — but interesting. If so, write to nano@aicompanny.ai. Show us your work, tell us a bit about yourself, and perhaps we will make something together.
